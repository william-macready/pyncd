**M4. Unsupported claim about why the spec chose the unique-model criterion, L299.**
Quoted: "Section 6 shows why this choice is forced by composition."
Problem: Section 6 shows no such thing. The spec (20.3) calls the criterion
"deliberately stronger" and says output-only determinacy "could be studied
separately; it is not silently substituted". It does not say composition
forces the choice. Example 4.25 shows only that relation-level equivalence is
incompatible with a denotation *already defined* by unique models, so the
argument is circular. Example 4.29 shows that the unique-model partial function
is itself non-compositional. An output-determinacy partial function fails
compositionality in the same way, since $t=t^2$ has models with different
outputs. And on the program of Example 4.15 output-only determinacy composes
*better*: $P_2;Q$ is output-determinate whenever $Q$ is. Nothing in the chapter
makes the choice "forced".
Fix: "The spec deliberately does not substitute output-only determinacy for
the unique-model criterion (Section 20.3). Section 6 shows that the span keeps
exactly the information (private multiplicity) that the two criteria
disagree on (Example 4.25)."

**M5. Remark at L144 and Exercise 6(c) at L562 point to a difference that does not exist.**
Quoted (L144): "but the duplicate occurrence is still there and still carries
its own definedness obligation."
Quoted (L562): "(c) Over the Boolean sort with OR, the collected value does
not change when a duplicate is deleted. Name one thing that can still differ
between the two programs. (Hint: Definition 4.4.)"
Problem: an exact duplicate has the same body, binders, guard and destination.
Its definedness obligation at $(s',\nu)$ is literally the same condition as
the original's at $(s,\nu)$. So $\mathrm{AdmEnv}$ (Definition 4.4) is
unchanged by deleting it. Over an idempotent $\oplus$, $\mathrm{Collect}$ is
unchanged too. Hence $\mathrm{Models}$, $\mathrm{AdmInput}$, $[\![P]\!]$ and the
span (up to isomorphism) are all identical. The hint therefore steers the
student to a false answer. What does differ is the occurrence set
$\mathcal{O}_P$ (its cardinality, so there is no relabeling bijection and
`collect_relabel` does not apply), and the operational trace (one more
CONTRIBUTE step per coordinate, Chapter 5).
Fix: change L144 to "Because OR is idempotent, deleting a duplicate does not
change any Boolean value, and since the duplicate's definedness obligation is
the original's, the models are unchanged. The occurrence set still differs, so
no relabeling law relates the two programs. Over a non-idempotent sort the
values differ." Change the hint in 6(c) to "(Hint: $\mathcal{O}_P$, and
Chapter 5's CONTRIBUTE count.)" Alternatively ask the student to *prove*
that $\mathrm{Models}$ is unchanged.

**M6. Provenance statement is false, L5.**

Problem: Part III contains no composition of programs. Definition 4.20

not part of the specification."

**m14. L476, "Model existence is not compositional".**

domains of $P$ and $Q$). Existence *is* determined compositionally by the
input-output relations, through relational composition:

$P$ on $\eta$ lies in the domain of $Q$'s relation. Exercise 8(d) relies on
exactly this, since "same relation" must be preserved by composition. The
slogan will mislead.
Fix: "*Model existence is not determined by the domains*: whether $P;Q$ has a
model depends on the boundary values of $P$'s models (it is determined by the
two input-output relations, composed as relations), not on whether $P$ and
$Q$ each have models somewhere."

## Verdict

The mathematics is sound. The equalizer and pullback presentation of models,
collection as a pushforward of scalar body values, the fixed-point reading
with no order and no least fixed point, the witness-span composition theorem
and its proof, and the monic-left-leg characterization are all correct. Every
spec number and every Lean snippet matches its source. Nothing contradicts the
specification, so there is no Critical finding. The defects are of three
kinds:

- **Broken internal cross-references** (M1-M3, plus m13(b)), apparently from
  renumbering.
- **Unsupported or misleading claims** about the spec's rationale (M4) and
  about Boolean duplicates, with an exercise hint that leads to a false answer
  (M5).
- **A false provenance statement** (M6) that presents Section 6, and the
  chapter's own one-sided strengthening of Proposition 4.27, as if they had
  the specification's authority.

The minor items are precision fixes to proofs and claims (m5-m13, m14,
m15), Lean and experimental-citation hygiene (m3, m10, m16, m19), notation
(m20), and length (m22). The chapter is ready once M1-M6 are fixed.

Severity counts: Critical 0, Major 6, Minor 22.
