## Major

**M1. Lines 57-60: a false statement about $\mathbf{FinSet}$, corrected only in the next paragraph.**
Quoted: "Isomorphic objects are not identified: two axes with the same extent are
different objects when their identities differ." In $\mathbf{FinSet}$ the objects
are sets, and $[n_a]=[n_b]$ is one object when $n_a=n_b$. Lines 62-68 then say
"The last two sentences need a precise reading" and move identity to the shape.
A reader who stops at the first paragraph learns a false fact about the central
invariant. ("Isomorphic objects are not identified" is also vacuous: no category
identifies isomorphic objects.) Fix: delete the two sentences at lines 57-60 and
state the result of lines 62-73 directly: "The coordinate set $[n_a]$ forgets the
axis identity: two axes with equal extents have the same coordinate set. Identity
lives in the shape, and every map between coordinate sets must come from an
explicit index expression, never from equal extents."

**M2. Summary (lines 5-7) and lines 416-418 contradict lines 683-691 about which operations are shadows of the triple.**
Line 5: "the three einsum operations (reindex, sum over a fibre, pointwise
product) are the shadow of an adjoint triple of Kan extensions". Line 416-417:
"The three operations in every tensor program are restriction, sum over a fibre,
and product over a fibre." Lines 683-691: the shadow of $\Pi_f$ is the fibrewise
product $f_\otimes$, which "has no counterpart among the core constructors: the
Tensor Logic core reduces only with $\oplus$" (confirmed: `Expr.reduce` folds with
`ops.add` only). So line 417 is false for Tensor Logic. The summary names a
different third operation, the pointwise product of two families on one index
set, which the chapter never derives from $\Sigma\dashv f^\ast\dashv\Pi$ (its set
origin is the cartesian product $A\times B$ of families in $\mathbf{Set}^I$, not a
member of the triple). Fix: summary, "the two operations that move values between
coordinate sets (reindex and sum over a fibre) are shadows of restriction and of
its left adjoint $\Sigma_f$; the pointwise product is the semiring multiplication,
the shadow of the cartesian product of families". Line 416-418: "The operations
are restriction, sum over a fibre, and the pointwise product. The third adjoint,
product over a fibre, has no core constructor (Section 3)."

**M3. Lines 419-422 contradict the chapter's own honesty paragraph (lines 469-478) and categorical notes Section 4.1.**
Quoted: "a coder who implements 'sum over the contracted index' is implementing
$\Sigma_f$ and inherits its laws (functoriality, invariance under relabeling,
grouping over coproducts) for free." A loop summing reals implements $f_!$, not
$\Sigma_f$, and lines 474-477 say explicitly that "for general $K$ we do not
derive the laws of $f_!$ from Theorem 2.4. We prove them directly from the
commutative monoid laws". The categorical notes say the laws "do not establish a
general pullback/pushforward adjunction or Kan-extension construction". (Chapter 0
line 103 makes the same claim; fix it in the same way.) Fix: "a coder who
implements 'sum over the contracted index' is implementing $f_!$, the
decategorified shadow of $\Sigma_f$. The laws it obeys (functoriality, relabeling,
grouping) are the shadows of set-level isomorphisms, and Section 3 proves them for
$f_!$ directly from the commutative-monoid laws."

**M4. Lines 663-675: the "precise sense" in which Proposition 2.8 shadows Theorem 2.4 is misattributed.**
Quoted: "It is also the decategorified shadow of Theorem 2.4, in the following
precise sense... (It is the Frobenius form of the adjunction:
$\Sigma_f(A\times f^\ast B)\cong\Sigma_fA\times B$, followed by $\Sigma_!$ to the
point.)" Two problems. (a) The hom-set bijection of Theorem 2.4 does not
decategorify to the pairing identity. Its cardinality is the multiplicative
identity
$\prod_j|B(j)|^{|\Sigma_fA(j)|}=\prod_i|B(f(i))|^{|A(i)|}$. The pairing
$\langle u,w\rangle$ counts $\Sigma_!(A\times B)$, and the transpose identity is
the cardinality of Frobenius reciprocity plus $\Sigma_{!_J}\Sigma_f\cong\Sigma_{!_I}$.
(b) Frobenius reciprocity is not a "form of the adjunction". It is an additional
property of $\Sigma_f\dashv f^\ast$ that holds here by pullback pasting
(equivalently, distributivity of $\times$ over $\sqcup$ in $\mathbf{Set}$, which
is how the chapter actually proves the bijection). "Frobenius" is also never
defined. CONVENTIONS fact 12 requires calling the transpose identity the shadow
of the adjoint triple. That is acceptable as a slogan, but the "precise sense"
must be correct. Fix: "The literal hom-set bijection of Theorem 2.4 counts to a
multiplicative identity, $\prod_j|B(j)|^{|\Sigma_fA(j)|}=\prod_i|B(f(i))|^{|A(i)|}$.
The pairing identity is the count of a companion isomorphism, Frobenius
reciprocity $\Sigma_f(A\times f^\ast B)\cong\Sigma_fA\times B$ (both sides are
$\{(i,a,b)\mid a\in A(i),\ b\in B(f(i))\}$ up to tagging, by distributivity of
$\times$ over $\sqcup$), followed by $\Sigma_!$ to the point." Keep the displayed
bijection.

**M5. Proposition 2.9 (lines 703-705) states something the text then says is false.**
Quoted: "the two core constructors `tab` and `at` are the unit and counit
specialized to scalar bodies". Lines 767-771: "`tab` is not literally the unit; it
is the *transpose* operation of the adjunction... `at` is the counit precomposed

partial case)." Then delete the "precise reading" paragraph (lines 767-772), or
keep only its final sentence about totality.

**M6. Definition 2.11 (lines 910-914) is ill-typed as written.**

**M7. Proposition 2.13 (lines 1060-1071): the labelled statement is a definition; the theorem is outside it and is overstated.**

**M8. Lines 1228-1231: collection on $\eta_0$ is described as a property of the input.**
Quoted: "On the failing input $\eta_0$, the body of s4 is undefined and there is no

## Verdict

The mathematics in the proofs is correct line by line: Theorem 2.4, Propositions
2.7, 2.8, 2.9 and 2.12, the span-category argument and the $\mathrm{Lin}$
functoriality all check. Every Lean snippet, theorem paraphrase and cited name is
accurate. The running-example numbers are right throughout. The honesty line holds
at its three required points: no adjunction on $K$-families, cartesian product kept
apart from tensor product, and no sum over $\bot$. The defects are in what
surrounds the proofs. The summary and line 417 attribute to the adjoint triple an
operation the chapter itself says has no core counterpart (M2). One coder-facing
paragraph contradicts the chapter's own decategorification caveat (M3). The
"precise sense" of the transpose-identity shadow cites the wrong isomorphism (M4).
Three labelled or headline statements are false as written and are corrected only
afterwards (M1, M5, M6). Proposition 2.13 puts its content outside its statement
and drops the spec's proof-status qualification (M7). One remark about $\eta_0$
confuses an input with an environment (M8). Every exercise is solvable after the
m6 and m7 fixes. About a quarter of the exercises and roughly 120 lines duplicate
Chapter 0 or the chapter's own proofs. The fixes are local rewrites; no proof has
to change.

Severity counts: Critical 0, Major 8, Minor 18.
