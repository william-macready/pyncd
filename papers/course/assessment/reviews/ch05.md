## Findings

### Major

**M1. Line 131: the rule preamble binds $a=\mathrm{dst}(o)$ for PUBLISH as well, which drops empty-fibre publication.**
Quoted: "**Definition 5.6 (the step relation)** ... For $o=(s,\nu)$ and $a=\mathrm{dst}(o)$:",
followed by all three rules.

- In the spec, "For $o$ and $a=\mathrm{dst}(o)$" heads only Section 25.1 (CONTRIBUTE). The PUBLISH
  rule of Section 25.2 has no occurrence, and its $a$ ranges over all of
  $\mathrm{Addr}_{\mathrm{Def}}\setminus\mathrm{dom}\,\sigma$.
- Read literally, Definition 5.6 lets PUBLISH fire only at addresses that are the destination of
  some occurrence. An empty-fibre coordinate is never such a destination, so it could never be
  published. That contradicts line 141 ("An empty fibre makes PUBLISH enabled from the start") and
  spec 25.2. It would also make every program with an unwritten coordinate block, for example the
  spec 27.2 interior coordinate and the guarded $Y[1]$ of Exercise 7.
- Lean's `publication` constructor quantifies over any `t : P.Defined` and any `p`.

Fix: write "For CONTRIBUTE and UNDEFINED, $o=(s,\nu)\in\mathcal{O}_P$ and $a=\mathrm{dst}(o)$; for
PUBLISH, $a$ is any defined address." Alternatively, put the binding sentence directly above the
CONTRIBUTE rule only.

### Minor

**m1. Line 15: the motivating failure does not occur with text order on `Scorer`.**
Quoted: "The naive executor loops over statements in text order and does `Z[j] += ...` into a buffer
that later statements read. `Scorer` shows why that is wrong."

In text order, $s_1$ and $s_2$ both finish before $s_3$ reads $Z$, so the buffer is complete and the
answer is right. The half-collected read needs a different order, for example $s_2,s_3,s_1,s_4$.
Such a permutation is semantically equivalent (spec Proposition 19.2), and that is exactly why text
order cannot be the rule.

Fix: "loops over statements in source order ... Reorder `Scorer` as $s_2,s_3,s_1,s_4$, which has the
same meaning (statement order is not semantic), and $s_3$ reads $Z[0]$ after only $(s_2,0)$ ..."

**m2. Line 192: the proof of Proposition 5.8 proves finiteness, not the stated bound.**
Quoted: "Both sets are finite."

The bound $\lvert\mathrm{step}(c)\rvert\le\lvert U\rvert+\lvert\mathrm{Addr}_{\mathrm{Def}}\setminus\mathrm{dom}\,\sigma\rvert$
is claimed, but the counting step is left implicit.

Fix: end with "So $\mathrm{step}(c)$ is the image of a partial map defined on
$U\sqcup(\mathrm{Addr}_{\mathrm{Def}}\setminus\mathrm{dom}\,\sigma)$, which gives the bound."

**m3. Lines 230 to 232: "invariant" means different things in Chapter 0 and here.**
Quoted: "A subset $I\subseteq\mathsf{Conf}$ is an *invariant* when $\mathrm{Init}(P,\eta)\in I$ and
...". Chapter 0 Definition 0.24 names the transition-closed subset itself "an *invariant* (or
*subcoalgebra*)", with no initial-state condition. The chapter notes the difference, but the same
word is now used for two notions.

Fix: call Definition 5.13 an "invariant of $(P,\eta)$" or an "initial invariant", and keep
"invariant" in Chapter 0's sense.

**m4. Lines 247 to 257: the proof of Lemma 5.14 skips the preservation cases for untouched data.**
Three cases are not stated:

- item 5 for previously consumed occurrences under CONTRIBUTE, which holds because $\sigma$ is
  unchanged;
- item 4 at a published $b\ne a$ under CONTRIBUTE, which holds because $U$ shrinks and $\alpha(b)$
  is unchanged;
- the accumulator formula under PUBLISH, which holds because $\alpha$ and $U$ are unchanged.

All are one-liners. `consume_invariant` handles them explicitly.

Fix: add the sentence "Every other item is preserved because the data it mentions is unchanged or
$U$ only shrinks."

**m5. Line 259: the claim about smaller footprints is too broad.**
Quoted: "A smaller footprint would let the machine evaluate a body whose value is not yet stable."

Chapter 3 (end of Section 6) says the footprint is sufficient, not minimal. Pruning $(X,1)$ from
$X[0]\oplus(0\otimes X[1])$ is sound. Only the strict cases (`at`, a product with zero) are at
issue.

Fix: "Dropping the whole array of `at`, or the zero side of a product, would let the machine
evaluate a body whose definedness is not yet determined."

**m6. Line 306: PUBLISH's premise is only part of the hypothesis of (a)'s second clause.**
Quoted: "its premise is exactly the hypothesis of the second clause of (a)."

The second clause also needs $\rho\in\mathrm{AdmEnv}(P)$, which comes from $\rho$ being a model,
not from the PUBLISH premise.

Fix: "its premise supplies the finished-fibre hypothesis of (a)'s second clause; the model supplies
admissibility."

**m7. Line 332, Remark 5.19: the remark hides that part (i) is vacuous on cyclic programs.**
Quoted: "Theorem 5.18 holds for every well-formed program, cyclic ones included".

This is true. But by the chapter's own Exercise 8, a program with a dependency cycle never
succeeds, so part (i) is vacuous on cyclic programs. Only part (ii) and the scheduler-independence
reading have content there. Putting this fact only in a starred exercise invites the opposite
reading.

Fix: add one sentence: "By Exercise 8, success itself witnesses a certificate, so (i) has content
only for certifiable programs; its point is that the scheduler need not know or check the
certificate. Part (ii) applies to every program."

**m8. Lines 3, 33, 40, 41 and 381: terms are used before they are defined.**

- "lax morphism" appears in the summary, the Section 1 table and Remark 5.23. It is defined only in
  Definition 5.25 (line 414).

- $\bar\mu$ is used in Remark 5.23 (line 381) and Section 8.2 (line 432) but never named. The proof

**m11. Line 393: the wording says every writer, but only some writer reads a given dependency.**

**m14. Line 482, Remark 5.31: the illustration may be accepted by production as a scan.**
Quoted: "statements `T[0] = X[]` and `T[i+1] = T[i]` for $i\in[1]$ ... at tensor level $T$ reads $T$,
which production rejects outside the scan construct."

This program has exactly the shape of a scan: a base slot plus a recurrence from slot $i$ to slot

- At tensor level there is a cycle $A\to B\to A$ that is not a scan self-read, so production

- Coordinatewise, $r(X,())=0$, $r(A,0)=1$, $r(B,0)=2$, $r(A,1)=3$ is a certificate.

- The proofs of Lemmas 26.1 and 26.2, Theorems 26.3, 26.5 and 26.6, and Corollary 26.7 are
  complete.
- The certificate-free versus certificate-dependent split is drawn correctly and repeatedly.
